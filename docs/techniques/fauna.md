# Fauna

Animals bring a scene to life, and the official sets show how: they are tiny, expressive builds
from slopes, curved and round pieces, with a clear head, a clear posture and one or two
colour accents. The engine has the horse `4493c01` (with saddle `4491b`) and plenty of
round, curved and cheese-slope parts for building the rest. Official animal sets lean on
`7037`, `13547`, `4920`, `5852` curved and wedged bricks, ball joints, `35480` rounded plates
and `25269` quarter tiles; see `part-vocabulary.md`. Parts the engine lacks need catalogue
additions; stick to what it can place until then.

## Principles

1. **Posture sells the animal**: a horse's arched neck, a cat's curved back, a bird's
   forward-leaning breast. A straight stack of bricks is a block, not an animal.
2. **Head, body, limbs, tail** each get a distinct shape and a colour change at the join
   (white socks, a dark nose, a pale belly).
3. **Build small animals sideways** (SNOT) so the body has curves: side-stud bricks `87087`,
   `11211`, `30414` as the ribcage, with slopes and curved slopes as the shoulders.
4. **Eyes and details**: two 1 x 1 round tiles `98138` in black or white on the head, or a
   dark dot on a side stud; a nose in a darker colour, ears as cheese slopes `54200`.
5. **Scale**: the animal fits its scene (a horse is about 2 wide x 10 long x 7 tall in the
   engine; cats and dogs 1-2 wide x 4-6 long; birds 1-2 wide x 2-4 long).

## Placement and behaviour

- **Show behaviour**: a horse tied at a trough, a dog by a door, chickens in a yard with a
  coop, ducks at the pond edge, a cat on a windowsill, birds on a roof ridge, a swan on the
  lake, a fish in the pond (lighter trans pieces).
- **Groups of odd numbers** (three ducks, five sheep) with varied heading; never in a line.
- **Habitat**: nests, hay, a trough, a kennel, a pen of fence `33303` / `3185`, a pond with
  reeds, a stable with a half-door, a perch.
- **Vehicles with animals**: team of horses in front of a wagon or stagecoach (see
  `vehicles.md`), a donkey with panniers.
- **Wildlife** in landscapes: a deer at the forest edge, a fox near a rock, butterflies over
  flowers, a heron on the bank, a squirrel on a trunk.

## Fantasy and mascot creatures

- Dragons, owls, gryphons and giant statues use the same principles at larger scale: a clear
  silhouette, two to three colour families, a small bright accent (eyes, flames).
- Statues (lions on gate posts, stone eagles) are built in `71` / `72` with `151` highlights
  from stacked slopes and curved bricks, standing on a plinth.

## Checklist

- Posture and silhouette recognisable from the side.
- Head, body, limbs and tail have distinct shapes and colour accents.
- Eyes, nose and ears present.
- Placed with a reason and a habitat; groups in odd numbers.
- Scale fits the scene.
