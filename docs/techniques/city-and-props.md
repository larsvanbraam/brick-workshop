# City, street and props

A street build wins on texture and life: layered pavement, furniture, signs, things that spill
out of doors, and interiors visible through the glass. Props are what makes a scene feel
inhabited. Use the lists below as a menu: pick at least eight from different groups, vary
their number and placement, and place each where it makes sense.

## The street cross-section

From the building outwards, front to back:

1. **Doorstep and threshold**: a step or slope, a mat tile, a lantern.
2. **Pavement**: tiles `3070b` / `3069b` / `63864` / `2431` in `71`, with `19` or `28` borders
   and some open studs (open studs only where props stand). Mix a few darker tiles for wear.
3. **Curb**: one plate higher than the road, a run of `72` or `71` 1 x 4 plates or tiles.
4. **Gutter and drain**: a grille tile `2412b` in `0` or `72`.
5. **Road**: `72` plates and tiles (a few `71` patches), `15` markings: a dashed centre line
   and a crossing of tiles; asphalt cracks as 1 x 1 `0` tiles.
6. **Opposite curb and pavement**, trees in pits, bollards.

Other street surfaces: cobbles from round tiles `98138` / `14769` in `71` / `72`, a gravel
courtyard, brick paving in `70` / `84`, a sandy main street (`19` / `28` with ruts), a
boardwalk (`70` / `308` plates, posts, a step).

## Spills: things that come out of doors

- Crates and barrels (barrel: `3941` + round plates + a band of a second colour), sacks
  (`3004` tan), a pile of logs (`30137`), a pallet of 2 x 8 plates.
- A café: round tables from `4150` / `14769` on a post, chairs, an umbrella (cones `3943b`).
- Flower buckets, hay bales, a ladder, a coat rack, a sandwich board.
- A wanted poster or a notice on the wall: a tile with a pictogram.

## Signs without lettering

The catalogue has no lettering. Use:

- A framed board (a tile in the shop colour in a frame of 1 x 1 bricks) with a
  **pictogram**: a loaf, a key, a bottle, a boot, a fish, a book, a mortar, an anvil.
- A **band of dark and light blocks** in a rhythm (as a lettering-like frieze).
- Shape and colour: a barber's pole as a red/white stack of round bricks; a pub's sign
  hanging in a bracket; a pharmacy cross in `3` / `2`.

## Interiors through the glass

Visible interiors are what turn a facade from a model into a place:

- **Shop**: counter (1 x 4 bricks with a tile top), shelves of 1 x 1 coloured bricks, a
  till (a 1 x 1 brick with a tile), a hanging lamp, goods near the window.
- **Café / pub**: a bar with stools, a back shelf of bottles (`3062b` in colours), tables,
  barrels, a fireplace.
- **Saloon**: a bar, a piano (a `3010` box with a black/white tile strip), tables, a stair
  to a balcony.
- **Home**: a table, a chair, a bed with a coloured blanket, a stove, a bookshelf of
  1 x 1 spines, a rug of tiles.
- **Library / museum**: shelves, a globe, displays on plinths, a skeleton.
- **Workshop / forge**: an anvil (black stack), a hearth (red / orange trans), tools on a
  wall rack (`3633` + clips), a bellows.
- **Station**: a ticket desk, a bench, a luggage trolley, a clock.

Keep interiors a few studs deep and visible from the street; colour them in a way that
contrasts with the facade.

## Market stalls

1. Four posts (1 x 1 `308` or `43888` columns), a counter, a back panel.
2. A **striped awning**: alternating `4` / `15` slopes `3040b`, overhanging 2 studs.
3. Goods: coloured 1 x 1 plates and bricks for fruit, a flower display (`24866`, `32607`),
   fish (see `fauna.md`), cloth rolls.
4. Dress the ground: crates, a barrow, a sign, a cat.
5. A pair of stalls is better than one; vary awning colours.

## Town infrastructure

- **Fountain**: round basin `74611` / `11213` in `71`, a pillar of round bricks, trans-light
  blue splash pieces, a tile edge in `151`.
- **Statue**: a plinth, a figure of stacked slopes, a laurel (`2417`).
- **Well**: ring of curved bricks / `2577`, a roof on posts, a bucket (a 2 x 2 round brick
  `3941` with a bar).
- **Bridge**: arch `3308` / `15254`, parapets `3633`, lamps at the ends.
- **Clock tower**, **bandstand**, **gazebo**: roof over posts, railings, bunting.
- **Walls and gates**: a low stone wall `71`, a gateway with posts and finials `4589`.
- **Station, harbour, quay**: platform edge with a yellow line, bollards, crates, a crane.

## Making a street read as a row

A row of buildings varies plot by plot: height, colour, rooflines, setbacks. Put the tallest
on a corner, vary ground-floor use (shop, café, bank), and stagger the shop awning colours.
Use an alley or a cobbled corner to break the rectangle. Each building has its own door,
its own dormer layout, its own chimneys.

## Lighting the scene

- Wall lanterns at doors, lampposts along the road (on the pavement by the curb, never beside a
  window or against a wall), lit windows (trans yellow `14` / trans orange `25` panes), a
  glowing fireplace.
- Tiny warm accents against cool grey stone read as life.

## Checklist

- Street layered: road, gutter, curb, pavement, façade.
- At least eight props from different groups, in varied counts.
- Pictogram signs, not blank boards.
- Interior visible through at least one shop window and the door.
- Things spill out of doors; there is somewhere people sit.
- Something is living: a tree, flowers, a cat, a bird, a horse.
- Neighbours differ in height, colour and roofline.

## Measured in official models

- Town and modular sets put 3 to 4% of pieces into plants and about 2 to 3% into props; the
  rest is structure and detail. Spend props on a few, big, well-placed items.
- 38-56% of pieces are small (1 x 1 and 1 x 2).
- Street sets rely on grille tiles `2412b` (drains, vents) and plate-and-tile paving at
  1.5 plates and tiles per brick.
